Executive summary

Market. Nigeria has about 157M internet subscriptions and 57% broadband penetration (NCC, 2026). Android holds 84% of phones, mostly Tecno, Infinix and itel. WhatsApp is on 95% of smartphones and TikTok is now the largest social platform. Nigerian-made games average a 1.91★ rating on Google Play, so the quality bar is low.

Gap. Local trivia today means cash game shows on a fixed schedule (Quiz Money, YTrivia) or once-a-day editorial puzzles (Zikoko GridLocked). Nobody owns "Naija trivia with my own friends, whenever I want."

Product. It has three modes:

  1. Async 1v1 duels: 3 rounds × 3 questions, played in turns.
  2. Live party rooms: up to 50 players; guests join from a WhatsApp link in the browser, no install needed.
  3. Daily Sabi Challenge: a Wordle-style shareable grid.

Categories are Slang & Pidgin, Nollywood, Music, Food and Mixed Naija. The game is built for budget Android phones and tight data plans: under 25 MB download, under 1 MB per match, and a Data Saver mode.

Business. Revenue comes from rewarded ads, coins and cosmetics, the Sabi Pass subscription (₦1,200/month), and brand-sponsored packs, which carry the most upside. At about 250k MAU the modelled revenue is about ₦22M a month, around break-even for a 6-person team if sponsorship works.

Compliance by design. There is no cash, no wallet and no paid entry. Google Play bans paid-entry prize games, and since November 2024 Nigeria regulates games of chance state by state. Prizes, if any, are sponsor-funded, free to enter and paid as airtime. The game follows NDPA/GAID with a 13+ rating and a restricted mode with guardian consent for 13–17s.

Build. React Native (Expo), a Preact web guest client, Node/Fastify API, Colyseus realtime, PostgreSQL and Redis on AWS. A team of about 5 reaches public launch in about 21 weeks for roughly ₦81M (~US$61k) including launch marketing. A leaner version costs about ₦40–45M.

1. Executive summary

  1. The audience is big, online and on Android. Nigeria passed 57% broadband penetration in July 2026 (124.4M broadband subscriptions) and recorded about 157M internet subscriptions in May 2026. Android holds 84% of mobile OS share, and the phones are mostly Tecno, Infinix and itel. We build for a ₦150k–₦250k Android phone with 3–4 GB RAM first.
  2. Data is cheap enough, but people still count megabytes. A 30-day 1 GB bundle costs about ₦800 after the 2025 tariff increase. Our budget is under 1 MB per 10-question match and under 25 MB for the first install.
  3. The social layer is WhatsApp, and culture spreads on TikTok. WhatsApp is installed on 95% of Nigerian smartphones. TikTok is now the largest social platform in Nigeria, and 66% of urban 18–34s use it regularly. Invites go out over WhatsApp and marketing runs on TikTok.
  4. No one owns "Naija trivia with friends" yet. Current local players are either cash-prize live shows (Quiz Money, the earlier YTrivia) or once-a-day editorial puzzles (Zikoko's GridLocked). There is no well-made, social, turn-based or party-room trivia game built entirely around Nigerian culture.
  5. The biggest risk is legal, and we can avoid it by design. Google Play bans games that "accept money in exchange for an opportunity to win a physical or monetary prize." The Supreme Court's November 2024 ruling also moved lottery and gaming regulation to the 36 states. The game will never take money for a chance at a prize. It makes money from ads, cosmetics, a subscription and sponsored packs. Any prizes are sponsor-funded and free to enter.

6. Regulatory scan (not legal advice; confirm with Nigerian counsel)

6.1 Gaming, lottery and prizes

  • Supreme Court, 22 Nov 2024 (AG Lagos State & Ors v. AG Federation & Ors, SC/1/2008): the National Lottery Act 2005 was nullified, and states alone may regulate lotteries and games of chance. Operators face per-state licensing (Mondaq).
  • Trivia is a game of skill. Risk only appears when users pay to enter for a chance at a prize (a "wager").
  • Design rule: never charge an entry fee for a prize, never sell in-app items that improve prize odds, and never pay cash prizes from users' money. Sponsored prizes are "free to enter, no purchase necessary" and come with official rules shown in the app.

6.2 Google Play policy (Real-Money Gambling, Games and Contests)

  • Prohibits "games that accept money in exchange for an opportunity to win a physical or monetary prize."
  • Prohibits participating with real money, "including in-app items purchased with money", to obtain a prize of real-world value.
  • Implication: IAP is fine for cosmetics, hints and subscriptions, but none of it may affect prize eligibility. Sponsored-prize events must be separate from purchases and have published rules.

6.3 Data protection: NDPA 2023 and GAID 2025

  • The NDPA General Application and Implementation Directive (GAID) took effect in September 2025 and replaced the NDPR and its 2020 framework.
  • Obligations that apply to us:
    • Appoint a DPO (in-house or outsourced).
    • Publish a GAID-compliant privacy policy.
    • Run a DPIA before profiling or processing children's data.
    • Do a compliance audit within 15 months of starting, then annually.
    • Register with the NDPC if we are a "data controller of major importance" (likely once we pass a meaningful user count, inferred).
    • Cross-border transfers (e.g. EU/US cloud) need a lawful basis: adequacy, standard contractual clauses, or another basis.
  • Children: under NDPA s.31 a child is anyone under 18. Their data needs parental or guardian consent with age verification. Decision: v1 is rated 13+, users under 18 get a restricted profile (no public chat, no ads personalisation, no prizes), and full accounts are 18+. We will run a DPIA for the under-18 flow.
  • Breach notification: the NDPA requires notifying the NDPC within 72 hours of becoming aware of a breach (NDPA s.40). Confirm with counsel.

6.4 Consumer and marketing

  • Advertising Regulatory Council of Nigeria (ARCON) pre-approval applies to paid ads in Nigerian media. Brief influencers to use the "#ad" disclosure.
  • The NCC Do-Not-Disturb (DND) rules affect promotional SMS. Use transactional routes only for one-time passwords (OTP).

02 · Product Requirements Document (PRD)

Product: Who Sabi? (working title), Naija trivia with friends Owner: Product lead · Version: 1.0 · Date: 14 Oct 2026 Related: 01 Research · 03 Game Design · 06 TRD

1. Problem statement

Nigerians love arguing about who knows Naija culture best: which Nollywood line came from which film, what a new slang word means, which artist sang a hook, and whose mum's egusi is right. That banter happens on WhatsApp and X, but no game turns it into a fair, fun contest you can play with your own friends.

  • Global trivia apps (Trivia Crack, QuizUp clones) don't know Nigerian culture.
  • Local trivia products are cash game shows on a fixed schedule (Quiz Money, YTrivia). They carry regulatory and trust baggage and you can't play them with your own people whenever you want.
  • Nigerian-made games on Google Play average 1.91★, so the bar for quality is low.

2. Vision

"Settle the argument." Who Sabi? is the quickest way to prove you know Naija better than your friends, in a 2-minute async duel or a 10-minute party round in your WhatsApp group.

  • User-generated questions going live without moderation (submissions are moderated).
  • Desktop app (a web join link exists only for party mode).

6. User stories & acceptance criteria (MVP)

Onboarding

US-1: As a new user, I can play my first round within 30 seconds of opening the app, without signing up.

  • AC1: First launch goes splash → 3-question "taste test" (one each from Slang, Food, Music) → result → prompt to save progress.
  • AC2: Guest progress is kept on the device and merged into the account when the user signs up.
  • AC3: Time to first question is ≤ 30 s at p75 on a Tecno Spark 10 (or similar) over 3G.

US-2: As a user, I can sign in with phone OTP or Google.

  • AC1: OTP is 6 digits, valid for 5 minutes, max 5 sends per number per day, with an SMS fallback to WhatsApp OTP (optional v1.1).

6.3 Difficulty recalibration (Elo-style item response)

  • Each question has a difficulty rating d_q (starts from the writer's level: 1 → 1200 … 5 → 1800).

  • Questions with < 5% or > 97% correct after 300 answers are flagged for editorial review (too hard or too easy, or possibly a wrong answer key).

2. Editorial principles (the "Question Bible")

2.1 The 10 rules

  1. One true answer. If two options could be argued as correct, rewrite it. For slang with several meanings, ask "Which is the MOST common meaning of…"
  2. Verifiable. Every factual question has a source_url in the CMS (Nairametrics, Pulse, The Native, Grammy.com, BusinessDay, NBS, Wikipedia as a last resort). Slang questions cite at least two usages, such as a tweet, a Zikoko article or a song lyric.
  3. Time-stamped. Anything that can change ("current", "latest", "highest-grossing", "most streamed") gets an "as of " in the text and a valid_until date.
  4. Short. Question ≤ 120 characters, options ≤ 30 characters each. Long text loses on a 15 s timer.
  5. Plausible distractors. All wrong options must be believable to someone who doesn't know: same type, similar length, and no joke options except in "Funny" packs.
  6. No tribal, religious or political baiting. No questions that mock an ethnic group, religion, region, gender or disability. No partisan politics, no living politicians as punchlines. History is fine when neutral and factual (e.g. independence date).
  7. Family-safe by default. Questions with mild innuendo or alcohol themes are flagged family_safe=false and excluded from Family mode and under-18 accounts.
  8. Respect the dead and the bereaved. No questions about the circumstances of a celebrity's death.
  9. Spell names right. Use each artist's own styling (e.g. "Ayra Starr", "Asake", "2Baba"). Keep a house name dictionary in the CMS.
  10. Represent all of Nigeria. Within each category, aim for at least 25% of content from outside Lagos (North, East, South-South, Middle Belt). Track it with the region_tag field.

2.2 Voice

  • The question is plain and clear; the personality lives in the "Did you know?" fact and the feedback copy.
  • Pidgin is welcome in Slang questions and feedback. Factual questions use standard English so they can be read quickly.

4. Functional requirements

4.1 Identity & accounts

  • FR-AUTH-1: Guest account created on first launch (device_id + server-issued guest JWT).
  • FR-AUTH-2: Phone OTP. E.164 normalisation (+234), 6-digit code, TTL 5 min, max 5 sends/day/number, max 10/day/device, max 5 verify attempts per code. Use the Android SMS Retriever API with an app hash in the SMS text.
  • FR-AUTH-3: Google / Apple sign-in using ID-token verification on the server.
  • FR-AUTH-4: Guest-to-account merge keeps the guest's stats, coins and duels. Conflict rule: if the phone already has an account, guest progress is merged into it and coins are added.
  • FR-AUTH-5: Account deletion completes within 30 days (NDPA right to erasure). Matches are anonymised, not deleted, so opponents' history stays valid.
  • FR-AUTH-6: Age band captured at signup. Under 13 is blocked. 13–17 sets restricted=true.

4.2 Content service

  • FR-CON-1: Questions are stored with options (array of 2–4), correct_index, category, subcategory, difficulty (1–5), d_q rating, media refs, family_safe, valid_from/valid_until, region_tag, status (draft → in_review → approved → live → suspended → retired), and author.
  • FR-CON-2: Question selection for a player: exclude anything the player saw in the last 30 days (using Redis bloom filters per user, with Postgres seen as a fallback), filter by category, family-safe flag and media mode (Data Saver), and pick by target difficulty with weighted randomness.
  • FR-CON-3: Never send correct_index to the client before the answer is submitted (except offline Practice packs).
  • FR-CON-4: Report intake, with auto-suspend thresholds from the content doc.
  • FR-CON-5: Nightly jobs: difficulty recalibration, expiry of valid_until, re-bucketing, and flagging outliers.
  • FR-CON-6: CMS bulk import from CSV (format = content/seed-questions.csv), with validation and dedup check.
  • FR-CON-7: When a live question is corrected, matches from the last 14 days that used it are re-scored (async job) and affected players are notified.

4.3 Duel service (async)

  • FR-DUEL-1: A state machine (see §5.2) runs on the server. Every transition is idempotent and keyed by (duel_id, round, player).
  • FR-DUEL-2: Both players get identical questions in a round. Options are shuffled per player with a deterministic seed hash(duel_id, user_id, question_id).
  • FR-DUEL-3: Turn deadline 48 h. A BullMQ delayed job handles expiry and sends reminder pushes at T-24h and T-4h.
  • FR-DUEL-4: Answer submission is per question (streamed). The server stores served_at and answered_at (see timing §5.3).
  • FR-DUEL-5: Offline tolerance: if the network drops during a question, the client keeps a local answer record. If it re-sends within the grace window (question time limit + 10 s), the answer is accepted with timing clamped to the limit (no speed bonus).
  • FR-DUEL-6: Random matchmaking: a Redis queue per rating bucket (±200, widening by 100 every 10 s, max 60 s, then fall back to a "Sabi Bot" with a disclosed bot label).

4.4 Party service (live)

  • FR-PARTY-1: Room code: 5 characters from ABCDEFGHJKMNPQRSTUVWXYZ23456789. Codes are unique among active rooms and reserved for 10 minutes after the room closes.
  • FR-PARTY-2: The room lifecycle is LOBBY → COUNTDOWN → QUESTION → REVEAL → LEADERBOARD → … → PODIUM → (LOBBY for play again) → CLOSED (after 10 min idle).
  • FR-PARTY-3: The server is authoritative for the question clock. It broadcasts question with ends_at in server time, and clients sync their clock with ping/pong offsets.
  • FR-PARTY-4: Reconnection: Colyseus allowReconnection(client, 60). Score is preserved. Questions missed while disconnected score 0.
  • FR-PARTY-5: Host migration: if the host disconnects for more than 60 s, the longest-connected player becomes host.
  • FR-PARTY-6: Rooms are capped at 20 (free) or 50 (Sabi Pass host). Spectators (v2) are unlimited and read-only.
  • FR-PARTY-7: Guests from the web join with a nickname. They get a guest JWT scoped to party:{roomId} only. Nickname profanity filter (EN + Pidgin + Yoruba/Igbo/Hausa word lists).

4.5 Daily Challenge

  • FR-DAILY-1: A job at 23:00 WAT picks tomorrow's 7 questions (one per category plus a hard "boss"), excluding questions used in a Daily in the last 365 days. Editors can override in the CMS up to 22:00.
  • FR-DAILY-2: One attempt per user per day, enforced by a unique (user_id, daily_id) constraint.
  • FR-DAILY-3: Percentile computed in real time from a Redis sorted set; state boards use one sorted set per state.
  • FR-DAILY-4: Streak logic in WAT (Africa/Lagos, UTC+1, no DST), with one streak freeze per week.

4.6 Social

  • FR-SOC-1: Friend graph (mutual) with request/accept. Recent opponents are suggested.

  • FR-SOC-2: Contact discovery uses k-anonymity prefix matching, because phone numbers are too guessable for a plain hash to protect them.

    1. The client normalises each contact to E.164 and computes SHA-256(phone).
    2. The client sends only the first 20 bits of each hash.
    3. The server returns the full hashes (and user IDs) of registered, discoverable users whose hash shares those prefixes. Each prefix matches many possible numbers.
    4. The client matches full hashes locally.

    The server never receives or stores the user's contact list. Users can opt out of being discoverable (Settings → Privacy).

  • FR-SOC-3: Deep links: https://whosabi.ng/d/{token} (duel invite), /r/{CODE} (party), /u/{username} (profile). Android App Links and iOS Universal Links. Deferred deep links use the Play Install Referrer API (Android) and a clipboard-free server fingerprint fallback (iOS, short-lived).

  • FR-SOC-4: Share cards are rendered on device (react-native-view-shot), and the link preview uses an OG image rendered server-side (Satori/resvg) and cached on the CDN.

4.7 Economy & monetisation

  • FR-ECO-1: Coins use a double-entry ledger (ledger_entries). The balance is derived from the ledger and cached. Every grant or spend has an idempotency key.
  • FR-ECO-2: Rewarded-ad rewards are granted only on AdMob server-side verification (SSV) callback, never on a client claim.
  • FR-ECO-3: IAP: RevenueCat webhooks → entitlement table. The client reads entitlements from our API, not directly.
  • FR-ECO-4: Purchases never affect prize eligibility. Sponsored-prize events check event.purchase_independent = true, and the code path has no access to entitlements.
  • FR-ECO-5: Remote-configurable prices and rewards.

4.8 Notifications

  • FR-NOT-1: Types: your_turn, duel_expiring, duel_result, friend_joined, daily_live, league_recap, party_invite, content_correction.
  • FR-NOT-2: Quiet hours 22:00–07:00 WAT (except party invites from friends). Max 4 non-turn pushes a day per user.
  • FR-NOT-3: Pidgin or English templates, based on the user's language setting.

4.9 Admin CMS

  • Roles: writer, fact_checker, editor, moderator, liveops, admin, support.
  • Question CRUD with workflow, CSV import, media upload (auto-converted to WebP/Opus and size-checked), pack builder, Daily scheduler, event/sponsor campaign setup, report queue, user lookup (support), ban/mute, audit log of every admin action.

5. Key technical designs

5.1 Question delivery payload (duel round)

{
  "round_id": "r_01J...",
  "questions": [
    {
      "qid": "q_01J...",
      "seq": 1,
      "type": "mcq4",
      "text": "Who directed The Wedding Party (2016)?",
      "options": ["Funke Akindele", "Kemi Adetiba", "Kunle Afolayan", "Niyi Akinmolayan"],
      "media": null,
      "time_limit_ms": 15000
    }
  ]
}

Questions in a round are revealed one at a time: POST /duels/{id}/rounds/{n}/questions/{seq}/serve returns a single question and records served_at. This prevents a player from looking up all three at once.

5.2 Duel state machine

CREATED ──(A plays R1)──▶ WAIT_B_R1R2 ──(B plays R1,R2)──▶ WAIT_A_R2R3 ──(A plays R2,R3)──▶ WAIT_B_R3 ──(B plays R3)──▶ [TIEBREAK?] ──▶ COMPLETE
   │                │                      │                     │
   └──── expiry ────┴──────── expiry ──────┴────── expiry ───────┴──▶ FORFEIT (winner = waiting player)
DECLINED (B declines before playing) · CANCELLED (A cancels before B plays)

5.3 Fair timing (server-authoritative with latency tolerance)

  • served_at_s: when the server sent the question. recv_at_s: when the server received the answer.
  • The client reports client_elapsed_ms, measured from when the question became visible (after image load) to the tap.
  • The server computes server_elapsed = recv_at_s − served_at_s and an RTT estimate (median of the last 5 pings on this session).
  • Accepted elapsed = clamp(client_elapsed_ms, server_elapsed − rtt_est − 250ms, server_elapsed), then floored at 300 ms (human minimum).
  • If server_elapsed > time_limit + rtt_est + 2,000 ms, the answer counts as a timeout.
  • Live rooms use the same rule, with served_at = broadcast time.
  • Store all raw values for anti-cheat analysis.

5.4 Option shuffling

perm = seededShuffle([0..n-1], seed = xxhash64(match_id ‖ user_id ‖ qid)). The server stores the permutation index map. The answer is submitted as the displayed index, and the server maps it back.

5.5 Leaderboards

  • Redis sorted sets: lb:daily:{yyyymmdd}, lb:daily:{yyyymmdd}:state:{code}, lb:league:{league_id}:{iso_week}, lb:global:rating.
  • Nightly snapshot to Postgres for history. TTL 8 days on daily keys.

For a team of 3–6 engineers, one deployable API with clear internal modules (auth, content, duel, party-results, daily, economy, social, admin) is faster to build and operate than microservices. 6. Resilience

  • Realtime node crash: the room state snapshot in Redis (after each question) lets a new node rehydrate the room. Clients auto-reconnect using the room ID plus a session token. Target: under 10 s disruption.
  • API dependency failures: circuit breakers (SMS, RevenueCat). Push sends are retried by the queue with exponential backoff.
  • DB: Multi-AZ failover under 2 min. Writes are idempotent so clients can retry safely.
  • Graceful degradation order: ads → images → leaderboards → party → duels → login. Duels and login are protected the longest.
  • Kill switches via feature flags: ads, shop, Question Factory, party rooms, each separately.

7. Security architecture (summary; see doc 10)

  • WAF in front of everything. Rate limits per IP, device and user (Redis token bucket).
  • JWT (ES256) short-lived access tokens. Refresh tokens are rotated and stored hashed; reuse is detected.
  • Internal service calls (realtime → API) use mTLS or HMAC-signed requests.
  • Least-privilege IAM. RDS in private subnets. No public DB endpoints.
  • The admin CMS sits behind SSO (Google Workspace) with role-based access, IP allow-list and an audit trail.

8. Observability

  • Logs: pino JSON → CloudWatch → (optional) Grafana Loki.
  • Metrics: Prometheus/OpenTelemetry to Grafana Cloud. Dashboards: API RED, WS connections, room count, questions/s, OTP success rate by network, push delivery, ad fill, ledger balance drift.
  • Tracing: OpenTelemetry auto-instrumentation for Fastify, pg and ioredis.
  • Alerts: p95 latency > 500 ms for 5 min, 5xx > 1%, OTP success < 85%, WS disconnect spikes, queue lag > 2 min, ledger imbalance ≠ 0.
  • Synthetic probes: k6/Checkly from a Lagos vantage point (a device farm or a small VPS in a Lagos DC) every 5 min.

1. Conventions

  • Auth: Authorization: Bearer <access JWT> (ES256, 15 min). Guests get a guest JWT.

  • Errors (RFC 9457 problem+json):

  • Pagination: cursor based, ?cursor=…&limit=20.
  • Time: ISO-8601 UTC in payloads. The client converts to WAT for display.

4. Gaming, prizes & promotions compliance

4.1 Rules we follow everywhere

  1. No paid entry for prizes. No mode where coins, gems or money buy a chance at a prize.
  2. IAP can't buy prize eligibility or prize odds. Lifelines are disabled in prize events, and purchases are never checked in prize eligibility code (enforced by the purchase_independent CHECK).
  3. Prizes are sponsor-funded, free to enter, skill-based (top scores in a fixed question set), with official rules published (eligibility 18+, dates, prize, how winners are chosen, tie-breaks, excluded states if counsel advises).
  4. No cash wallet. Prizes are delivered as airtime or data top-ups to the verified phone number, or as physical merch.
  5. No chance mechanics tied to real value. No loot boxes with real-money purchase; cosmetic loot (if ever) shows its odds and stays cosmetic-only.

4.2 Why

  • Google Play policy (Real-Money Gambling, Games and Contests) bans "games that accept money in exchange for an opportunity to win a physical or monetary prize" and participation with "in-app items purchased with money" to obtain real-world prizes.
  • Nigeria: since the Supreme Court ruling of 22 Nov 2024, lotteries and games of chance are regulated by each state (e.g. Lagos State Lotteries and Gaming Authority). A free-entry skill contest sits outside "lottery" and "gaming" definitions in most regimes, but counsel must confirm for Lagos, FCT, Oyo, Rivers and Kano before the first prize event, and the event rules can exclude states if needed.
  • Sales promotions: brand-sponsored promotions may need approval from the relevant state lottery or gaming authority, or the Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission (FCCPC), depending on structure. The brand partner usually owns that compliance; our contract template says so.

5. Advertising & marketing compliance

  • ARCON: ads placed in Nigerian media (including digital and influencer content) need ARCON vetting. Budget time and fees into campaigns.
  • Influencers: a contract clause requires "#ad" or "#sponsored" disclosure.
  • SMS: OTP only, on a transactional route. No promotional SMS without opt-in, respecting NCC DND rules.
  • Ad content filters: block gambling, betting, alcohol (for under-18s), dating, crypto and political categories in AdMob for all users. Blocking betting ads protects the brand, since the game must not look like a betting app.

4. Specific test cases (high-risk areas)

4.1 Timing & fairness

  • TC-T1: client reports elapsed 200 ms while server elapsed is 4,000 ms → accepted elapsed ≥ server − RTT − 250 ms.
  • TC-T2: answer arrives at limit + RTT + 2.1 s → timeout, 0 points.
  • TC-T3: an image question's timer starts only after the image has rendered. On a slow network the image times out at 5 s, then the text-only fallback starts.
  • TC-T4: an app backgrounded during a question (phone call) counts as a timeout; a 10 s network drop gives a one-time void-and-replace.

4.2 Duel state machine

  • Every transition, including expiry at each state, forfeit winner logic, and the tiebreak path.
  • Concurrency: both players submit at the same time; a duplicate answer with the same idempotency key; a different key for the same question → 409.

4.3 Economy

  • A rewarded SSV callback replayed 5 times → one grant.
  • A purchase refund webhook → coins clawed back (the balance may not go negative: the rest is recorded as debt and blocks shop spending).
  • A lifeline debit fails mid-way → no effect applied (transactional).

4.4 Content

  • CSV import rejects: > 160 chars, correct index out of range, duplicate external_ref, missing source on a factual category (warning).
  • Auto-suspend at the report thresholds; a re-score job updates past duel scores and sends the notification.
  • valid_until expiry removes the question from serving at 00:00 WAT.

4.5 Time zone

  • Daily rollover at 00:00 WAT with device clocks set wrong (±3 h) → the server date decides.
  • Streak freezes across an ISO-week boundary.

4.6 Privacy

  • Account deletion: PII is cleared and opponents see "Deleted player".
  • Contact discovery: the server logs show only 20-bit prefixes, never full numbers.
  • A 13–17 account: ads are non-personalised, prize events are hidden, and there's no discoverability.

3. Customer support

  • Channels: in-app help centre (FAQs in EN and Pidgin), in-app ticket form (attaches app version, device and user ID automatically), email help@whosabi.ng, and an X DM watch.
  • Tooling: a helpdesk (Freshdesk or Zendesk free tier, or Crisp), linked to the CMS user lookup.
  • SLAs: first response within 24 h (P1 issues like a lost purchase within 4 h in working hours); resolution within 72 h.
  • Top macros: lost streak, purchase not received (check RevenueCat → re-grant), wrong question dispute, username change, account deletion, banned appeal, OTP not received (check the provider logs per carrier).
  • Compensation policy: the support role can grant up to 500 coins without approval; more needs an editor or product approval. Every grant goes through the ledger (reason='support_grant').

4. Moderation

  • Usernames & nicknames: automatic filter at creation plus user reports; moderators can force a rename.
  • Question Factory submissions: community votes, then editor approval; a rejected submission sends a friendly reason.
  • Prohibited: tribal or religious insults, sexual content, hate speech, doxxing, impersonating celebrities (verified-badge system for real creators in v2).

5. On-call & runbooks

  • One engineer on call each week with a backup; paging through Opsgenie or PagerDuty (free tiers) for SEV1/SEV2.
  • Runbooks (to write during S6):
    1. OTP failure on one carrier → switch the provider route.
    2. Realtime node unhealthy → drain and replace.
    3. DB failover.
    4. Bad question live → suspend + re-score.
    5. Ads causing crashes → disable the network or format through a flag.
    6. Push storm or duplicate pushes → pause the queue.
    7. Suspected breach → incident process (doc 10 §7).
    8. Daily not generated by 23:30 → manual publish.

6. Status & comms

  • In-app status banner controlled from the CMS ("Party rooms are having issues. Duels still work").
  • An X account for status updates. Post-mortems summarised publicly for major incidents.
